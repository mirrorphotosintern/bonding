# Try This — Design Context

> Status: working design context informed by founder direction, competitor review, `ParentLinks.csv`, and the requested Shaale-like mobile stack. The proposed visual thesis awaits founder confirmation in `COMPETITIVE_VISUAL_STRATEGY.md`.

## Design Context

### Users

- **Primary user:** A parent or caregiver of a child aged 3–10 who wants to be present but is tired, short on time, or out of ideas.
- **Secondary participants:** The child, siblings, grandparents, babysitters, and teachers who join the real-world activity without needing an account.
- **Context:** Transitional family moments—after school, before dinner, a rainy afternoon, a restaurant wait, travel, or a weekend pocket of time. The adult usually has one hand free and little patience for setup.
- **Job to be done:** Convert “we should do something together” into one safe, achievable, shared action within 30 seconds.
- **Emotional starting state:** Often depleted, guilty, scattered, or bored. The product must lower pressure, not imply that the adult is failing.
- **Emotional ending state:** “We did a real thing together.” The child feels seen and capable; the adult feels lighter, not evaluated.

### Brand Personality

Three words: **resourceful, warm, spirited**.

- **Resourceful:** Makes ordinary objects and tiny moments feel useful. Never demands a craft-store run or a Pinterest-ready home.
- **Warm:** Speaks caregiver-to-caregiver. Reassuring without sentimentality, expert without lecturing.
- **Spirited:** The activities feel surprising, kinetic, and a little mischievous. Joy comes from doing, not decorative confetti.

### Aesthetic Direction

**A compact family field kit.**

- Light-first because the app is used in kitchens, cars, parks, and daylight family spaces.
- Tactile visual language: instruction tabs, functional pencil marks, material labels, folded paper, restrained texture, and honest photography of hands beginning things.
- The interface should feel like passing one sturdy instruction card from the phone to the family, not browsing a content platform.
- The proposed signature is a Handoff Strip that transforms preparation into a glanceable Together state and explicitly cues the phone to leave the interaction.
- Avoid the increasingly generic cream/terracotta/editorial-serif direction used by close competitors.
- A single recommended activity dominates the home screen. Browsing is secondary.
- Color should separate modes—Make, Move, Think, Talk, Help, Perform—without turning the UI into a rainbow grid.
- Typography should feel practical and human, like a good museum family guide. Final font selection must follow a catalog review; do not default to Inter, DM Sans, Fraunces, Syne, or the other common AI-design reflexes.
- Motion is used for the transition from phone mode to the playable idea page, timers, turn-taking, and completion. No bounce, casino-like reward effects, or ambient animation that competes with the child.
- The child should never need to stare at the phone to participate. Large, glanceable prompts and optional audio are essential.

### Anti-references

- Not a TikTok feed of activities.
- Not a Pinterest board with high-prep perfection.
- Not a chore chart, behavior score, or parent-performance dashboard.
- Not a child-facing game that claims to reduce screen time while requiring more screen time.
- Not a pastel wellness app that treats play as therapy.
- Not loud generic edtech, glassmorphism, purple-blue gradients, identical card grids, or mascot-led gamification.
- Not a warm-cream lifestyle landing page with oversized editorial serif type.
- Not stars, streaks, currencies, reward pets, or confetti.

### Design Principles

1. **One good answer beats a feed.** Default to a single match with one-tap swaps.
2. **Make starting feel natural.** Give the family what they need without instructing them how to hold or abandon the phone.
3. **Make the child capable.** Give the child roles, choices, and room to alter the rules.
4. **Respect the adult's actual energy.** “Barely functioning” is a legitimate input, not a failure state.
5. **Show reality, not aspiration.** Ordinary materials, imperfect results, different homes, bodies, and family structures.
6. **Celebrate the shared act, not compliance.** No child scores, parent grades, public comparison, or broken-streak shame.
