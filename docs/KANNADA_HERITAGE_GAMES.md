# Kannada heritage games — first toddler collection

This collection was created for the 2026 AKKA hackathon demonstration. It is a
specific cultural collection inside the globally oriented Try This product; it
does not change the positioning of the complete catalog.

## Audience and presentation

- Designed first for a child around 18 months old and one grown-up.
- Kannada script is always shown with a Romanized reading aid.
- Traditional rhymes have household and regional variants. Cards say which
  version is being used instead of claiming a single canonical text.
- The physical action is the product: carrying, pointing, wiggling, tracing,
  pretend serving, and tickling. A future video can replace or supplement the
  generated illustration without rewriting the play card.

## Included games

1. **ಆನೆ ಬಂತೊಂದಾನೆ · Aane Bantond Aane** — the grown-up becomes a slow
   elephant while the toddler rides or uses the seated lap version.
2. **ಅವಲಕ್ಕಿ ಪವಲಕ್ಕಿ · Avalakki Pavalakki** — a counting-out chant tapped
   across five fingers.
3. **ಹೆಬ್ಬೆರಳಣ್ಣ · The Five Fingers** — a Kannada “Daddy Finger” family
   version naming and greeting all five fingers.
4. **ಊಟ ಯಾರಿಗೆ? · Oota Yaarige?** — prepare a pretend Kannada meal in the
   palm, feed the family, then finish with “ಕಳ್ಳ ಬಂದ!” and a gentle tickle.

## Sources and provenance

- The descriptive **Aane Bantond Aane** wording is documented by [Portland
  Kannada Koota's Kannada class](https://pkkkannadaclass.blogspot.com/2011/11/kannada-class-ii.html)
  and [Appu Series Kannada](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nJ-fQeFIil0). The
  product card notes that families may use their own regional wording.
- The short **Avalakki Pavalakki** form—“ಕಾಂಚನ ಮಿಣಮಿಣ / ಡಾಮ್ ಡೂಮ್ / ಟಸ್
  ಪುಸ್ / ಕೊಯ್ ಕೊಟಾರ್”—is documented as a traditional play chant by
  [Vishaya](https://vishaya.in/kathe/children-story/avalakki-pavalakki/)
  and other Kannada cultural writing.
- **Hebberalanna** and **Oota Yaarige** are explicitly labeled Try This family
  versions based on the founder-provided play sequences. They need founder
  video review before being described as a particular regional form.
